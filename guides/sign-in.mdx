title: "Sign a person in"
description: "Link a person who signs in to your app through id.pincast.fm, so your key can record their collects."
---

People sign in to your app with their Pincast account at id.pincast.fm. Your server runs the sign-in, then sends the person's ID token to `POST /people` once per sign-in. After that, your key can record their collects and claims.

The code below comes from the server in [Your first signed-in collect](/guides/signed-in-collect), which has the whole file. A test key can't link a real person, so these calls take a live key.

## Make your sign-in client

Open your app on the [dashboard](https://pincast.fm/developers/dashboard) and go to its **Sign-in** tab:

| Field | What it takes |
| :- | :- |
| **App name** | The name people see when they sign in. |
| **Sign-in addresses** | Where Pincast sends people after they sign in, one per line. Each is an exact https address on a real host name, with no wildcards. While your app has no live version, it can also be a localhost address. |
| **Sign-out addresses** (optional) | Where people land after they sign out, one per line, under the same rules. |
| **Logo address** | An https link to your logo image. |
| **Dark mode logo address** (optional) | An https link to a logo for dark backgrounds. |
| **Terms of use address** | An https link to your terms. |
| **Privacy policy address** | An https link to your privacy policy. |
| **Permissions** | What your app may read. **Name, username and picture** is ticked to start. |

Tap **Save and make client**. Your app becomes a Traditional app on id.pincast.fm: its sign-in runs on your server, next to your key. The page shows your client ID, and shows the secret once. Put these three values in your server's environment:

| Value | What it is |
| :- | :- |
| `PINCAST_SIGNIN_CLIENT_ID` | Your client id. Your ID tokens name it in `aud`. |
| `PINCAST_SIGNIN_CLIENT_SECRET` | Your client secret. Keep it on your server. |
| `PINCAST_SIGNIN_ISSUER` | `https://id.pincast.fm/oidc` |

To change a setting later, tap **Change settings** on the same tab. The form opens with your saved settings.

### Test on localhost

While your app has no live version, a sign-in or sign-out address can use `localhost`, `127.0.0.1` or `[::1]`, such as `http://localhost:3000/callback`. The host has to be one of those three, written exactly that way.

This tests the sign-in itself. Linking the person with `POST /people` still takes a live key, because a test key can't link a real person.

Remove localhost addresses before you go live. Once your app is live, a save that still holds one is refused until you take it out.

### Remove a permission

Untick a saved permission and save. That part of your app's sign-in stops working until you tick it again.

A permission the page doesn't offer stays on your client through every save. To remove one, email [support@pincast.fm](mailto:support@pincast.fm).

### Replace a secret

1. Tap **Make a new secret**. Your older secrets keep working, so nothing breaks while you deploy.
2. Put the new secret on your server and deploy it.
3. Tap **Turn off older secrets**. They stop working right away, and this can't be undone. If your server still uses one, people can't sign in until you deploy the new one.

If a secret leaked, do all three now.

## Send the person to sign in

Redirect the person to `https://id.pincast.fm/oidc/auth` with these parameters:

| Parameter | Value |
| :- | :- |
| `client_id` | Your client id. |
| `redirect_uri` | One of your registered sign-in addresses, exactly. |
| `response_type` | `code` |
| `scope` | `openid offline_access profile` |
| `state` | A random value you keep in the person's session and check when they return. |
| `code_challenge` | The base64url sha256 of a random verifier you keep in the session. |
| `code_challenge_method` | `S256` |
| `prompt` | `consent` |

```js
function startSignIn(req, res) {
  const session = sessionOf(req) ?? openSession(res)
  session.state = random(18)
  session.verifier = random(32)
  const url = new URL(`${ISSUER}/auth`)
  url.search = new URLSearchParams({
    client_id: env.PINCAST_SIGNIN_CLIENT_ID,
    redirect_uri: REDIRECT_URI,
    response_type: 'code',
    scope: 'openid offline_access profile',
    state: session.state,
    code_challenge: createHash('sha256').update(session.verifier).digest('base64url'),
    code_challenge_method: 'S256',
    prompt: 'consent',
  })
  res.writeHead(302, { Location: url.href }).end()
}
```

Ask for those three scopes and no others: id.pincast.fm refuses a scope your app wasn't given, with `invalid_scope`.

After signing in, the person sees a consent screen with:

- Your app's name and logo, beside Pincast's.
- The account they're signing in with.
- What your app asks for: "Your name, username, avatar, and other profile info".
- **Cancel** and **Authorize**.
- Links to your terms and privacy policy.

**Authorize** sends them to your `redirect_uri` with `code` and `state`. **Cancel** sends them back with `error` in place of a code.

## Exchange the code

From your server, post the code to `https://id.pincast.fm/oidc/token`. Your client id and secret go in HTTP Basic, and the verifier goes in the body:

```js
async function exchangeCode(code, verifier) {
  const client = `${env.PINCAST_SIGNIN_CLIENT_ID}:${env.PINCAST_SIGNIN_CLIENT_SECRET}`
  const res = await fetch(`${ISSUER}/token`, {
    method: 'POST',
    headers: {
      Authorization: `Basic ${Buffer.from(client).toString('base64')}`,
      'Content-Type': 'application/x-www-form-urlencoded',
    },
    body: new URLSearchParams({
      grant_type: 'authorization_code',
      code,
      redirect_uri: REDIRECT_URI,
      code_verifier: verifier,
    }),
    signal: AbortSignal.timeout(8000),
  })
  const tokens = await res.json().catch(() => ({}))
  if (!res.ok || !tokens.id_token) throw new Error(`The code exchange answered ${res.status}: ${tokens.error}`)
  return tokens
}
```

The answer holds `id_token`, `access_token`, `refresh_token`, `expires_in`, `scope` and `token_type`. The next step uses `id_token`.

## Link the person

Check `state` first, then exchange the code and send the ID token to [`POST /people`](/api-reference/people/link-a-person-who-signed-in-to-your-app), once per sign-in:

```js
async function finishSignIn(req, res, query) {
  const session = sessionOf(req)
  if (!session?.state || query.get('state') !== session.state) {
    return send(res, 400, '<p>This sign-in has expired. <a href="/signin">Sign in again</a>.</p>')
  }
  const { verifier } = session
  delete session.state
  delete session.verifier
  if (query.get('error')) return send(res, 200, '<p>You didn\'t sign in. <a href="/">Back</a>.</p>')

  const tokens = await exchangeCode(query.get('code'), verifier)
  // Once per sign-in: this links the person to your app, so your key can record their collects.
  const { person } = await api('POST', '/people', { token: tokens.id_token })
  const { username } = JSON.parse(Buffer.from(tokens.id_token.split('.')[1], 'base64url'))

  // A new session id at sign-in, so an id set before it can't carry over.
  sessions.delete(sidOf(req))
  openSession(res, { person, name: username ?? person })
  res.writeHead(303, { Location: '/' }).end()
}
```

`POST /people` answers with the person's id:

```json
{
  "app": "your-app",
  "person": "q8n2v5x7c1z4",
  "linkedAt": "2026-11-14T15:10:01.000Z"
}
```

Keep `person` in your session. Send it as `actor.id`, with `actor.source` set to `logto`, in each collect:

```js
  await api('POST', `/sets/${SET_ID}/collects`, {
    card: CARD,
    actor: { id: session.person, source: 'logto' },
    at: new Date().toISOString(),
  })
```

Sending a token for a person who's already linked answers the same link, so a returning person needs no special case. To move cards they collected as a guest, see [Your app's collection and Placecard's](/concepts/collections).

## What your app receives

The ID token is a JWT signed with ES384, and it lasts one hour. It holds:

| Claim | What it holds |
| :- | :- |
| `sub` | The person's Pincast id, the same value as `person`. |
| `name`, `username`, `picture` | Their profile. |
| `created_at`, `updated_at` | When their account was created and last changed. |
| `iss` | `https://id.pincast.fm/oidc` |
| `aud` | Your client id. |
| `iat`, `exp` | When the token was issued and when it expires. |

**Your app doesn't receive the person's email address.** The token has no `email` claim. If your app needs an address, ask the person for it in your app.

The token answer also carries a refresh token, because the scopes include `offline_access`. Recording collects doesn't need it. If you keep one, keep it on your server, like your secret.

Placecard reads only the token's issuer, subject, audience and times, and keeps none of the person's profile.

## When a call is refused

| Code | Status | What to do |
| :- | :- | :- |
| `token_other_app` | 403 | The token was made for another client, or your app has no sign-in client yet. Check `PINCAST_SIGNIN_CLIENT_ID` against your app's **Sign-in** tab. |
| `token_expired` | 400 | The token is past its hour. Have the person sign in again. |
| `token_invalid` | 400 | Send the `id_token`, unchanged. The answer's `data.fix` names the check that failed. |
| `signin_check_unavailable` | 503 | Wait the seconds in `Retry-After`, then send the same call again. |
| `person_not_signed_in` | 403 | A collect or claim came before the link. Send `POST /people`, then send the call again. |

[Errors](/api-reference/errors) lists every code.
